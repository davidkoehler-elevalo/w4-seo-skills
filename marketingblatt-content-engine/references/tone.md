# Tone & writing style — model: Pipedrive DE blog (binding)

The target voice for every Marketingblatt article, reverse-engineered from the Pipedrive German blog
(verified across the lead-scoring, "Was ist CRM?", and workflow-automation guides). Pipedrive's craft
is the model to replicate **exactly and consistently**, so every article reads in the same tone.

## The one adaptation (non-negotiable)
Pipedrive writes **"Sie"**. Marketingblatt writes **"ihr/euch"** (see [[brand-voice]], [[feedback-anrede-ihr]]).
Keep ihr/euch, Swiss `ss`, and no em dashes. **Everything else about Pipedrive's style, replicate.**

## The tone in one line
Authoritative yet accessible. Warm and instructional, never cold. Education-forward, plain-professional,
with the occasional idiom that makes it sound human. Helpful expert, not salesperson (the one sell comes
at the very end).

## How to open (article + sections)
Pipedrive opens one of two ways, both value/clarity-first:
1. **Relatable truth or pain**, then the turn. Pipedrive: *"Leads sind potenzielle Kunden. Doch wie
   jeder Vertriebler weiss, führt längst nicht jeder Lead zum Kaufabschluss."* → then the problem it
   solves.
2. **Crisp definition + immediate benefit.** Pipedrive: *"CRM ist die Abkürzung für Customer
   Relationship Management. Ein CRM-System hilft euch dabei, alle Kundendaten zentral zu bündeln."*

Sections open with a **direct statement** or a **rhetorical question** (*"Welche Position bekleidet der
potenzielle Kunde?"*). Never a slow wind-up.

## Sentence rhythm (reinforces [[readability]])
Vary the pace. Pair a short, punchy declarative with a medium explanatory one.
- Short: *"Lead Scoring ist hilfreich, aber auch recht knifflig einzurichten."*
- Medium: *"Je mehr Punkte ein Lead erhält, desto wahrscheinlicher wird er zum Kunden."*
Clarity beats complexity. Long nested sentences are off-style; split them.

## Talk TO the reader
Frequent, warm direct address in ihr-form:
- *"Ruft euch eure Buyer Persona in Erinnerung."*
- *"Stellt euch das so vor …"*
- *"Rechnet es einmal durch."*
- *"Ihr entscheidet selbst, welche Faktoren zählen."*

## Idioms and analogies — dosed, not forced
Pipedrive humanises with everyday German idioms and small analogies. Use **2 to 4 per article**, where
they fit naturally, never stacked:
- *"die Spreu vom Weizen trennen"*, *"aus dem Bauch heraus"*, *"auf Autopilot stellen"*,
  *"Staub ansammeln lassen"*, *"sich verzetteln"*.
These replace dry abstraction with a picture. Do not invent clumsy metaphors (see [[brand-voice]]
metaphor budget); borrow real, common ones.

## Teach with concretes
- **Worked examples** with a scenario and numbers: *"Angenommen, euer idealtypischer Kunde …"*
- **Comparative examples**: two contrasting cases side by side (Einkaufsleiterin vs. Personalchef).
- **Numbered step sequences** ("In fünf Schritten"), **tables/matrices**, **bulleted lists**.
- **Bold** the key term the first time it matters.
- Expand an acronym briefly on first use (*"Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL)"*); the full conceptual
  definition still lives in the FAQ.

## Signpost the flow
Move theory → practice → tools with explicit connectors:
*"Im zweiten Schritt …"*, *"Zum Schluss …"*, *"Nun legt ihr fest …"*,
*"Damit ist das Potenzial noch lange nicht ausgeschöpft."* The reader always knows where they are.

## Paragraphs
3 to 6 sentences, broken up by lists, examples, or a table. No walls of text.

## What this is NOT
Not stiff/corporate, not jargon-heavy, not hype ("katapultiert euer Business in neue Sphären" is
Pipedrive at its most salesy — we stay a notch drier). Warmth and clarity, backed by concrete
examples, is the whole game.

Layered on top of [[brand-voice]] (rules, budgets, AI-tells) and [[readability]] (sentence craft).
Where they overlap, all three must hold at once.
